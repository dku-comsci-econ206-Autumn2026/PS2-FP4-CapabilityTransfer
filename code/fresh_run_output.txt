Future market-share motive
  future(m) = beta*phi*K*m/(m+m0),  beta = 0.9, phi = 0.5, K = 44.4, m0 = 0.5
  a firm opens at k = 0 iff future(m) > L + PI_0*m, i.e. m in (0.101, 1.519)
  share of firms inside that window: 0.75
  at k = 0 the modal decision is (2) with share 0.68 -> 2 distill
  with the motive off, at k = 0 the modal decision is (1) with share 0.96 -> 1 closed

Thresholds
  disclosure needs  k*E_d > L + PI_0*m - beta*phi*K*m/(m+m0)
    at m = 1, motive on:   k > -2.60
    at m = 1, motive off:  k > (L + PI_0)/E_d = 10.92   (the familiar k > L/E_d = 2.56 holds only if pi = 0)
  sources are modal from  k = 3.45 (motive off)  and  k = 1.19 (motive on)
  innovation needs  k > (C_n - C_d)/(E_n - E_d) = 1.1591
  sources stay modal under indexed k = 4 with the motive on: share(4) = 0.95
  capping pays small-E and large-E releases alike, share(4) = 0.05

  Social loss  c_e*B + rho*max(0,T-T0) - v*F(T),  B = 100, rho = 0.01, v = 1, c_e = 0.02
    all distill    +2.00
    all train      +1.77
    t* = 0.35      +1.35
  A positive distillation share is efficient: it stops every firm from paying
  to re-derive the same result. Distillation is cheap, but never free, so the
  reward metric must be E.

  read as an all-pay auction: every bidder pays its own bid, so compute/source is the
  rent a rule dissipates per original release, prize spend is public spending, and
  source share is the share of releases that are original. Winner-take-all would hand
  the prize to the highest verified E, always an original release whenever any firm
  trains; our rule pays proportionally instead, so what separates the rules is the
  index, not the level or the allocation form.
